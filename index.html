How the score works
  • Every sit starts at 50. Each factor adds or subtracts points, and the detail view shows the math.
  • Time of day strong evidence. Dawn and dusk beat everything else. Midday sits only show up during the rut, at a penalty.
  • Rut timing strong evidence. In a Mississippi study of 48 collared bucks, they moved about 140 more yards per hour at peak rut. The North Jersey dates here are an estimate: seeking Oct 28 to Nov 9, peak Nov 10 to 22, tail to Dec 5, second rut Dec 6 to 18.
  • Wind direction is not part of the movement score, because it changes where deer can smell you, not whether they move. It shows as a separate tag for your blind: south is ideal, SE and SW are OK, E and W are iffy, and north is bad. When picking your best 3, it only breaks close calls, worth up to 6 points.
  • Hourly chart. Movement peaks around sunrise and sunset and drops midday, with the midday floor rising during the rut. That curve follows GPS-collar studies: Mississippi bucks bedded about 21% of the time within an hour of dawn or dusk, versus 34% across the whole day. Weather and your sightings then raise or lower each hour.
  • Temperature drop, wind speed moderate evidence. Colder than recent days means more daytime movement. High wind means less.
  • Barometric pressure and rain weak to moderate evidence. Effects show up mostly at the extremes, like pressure rebounding after a front or rain that just stopped.
  • Your sightings. Daylight sightings in the last 45 days add up to 8 points to sits at the same time of day. Camera photos at night don't count, because you can't hunt at 2 AM.
  • Moon phase no measurable effect. It's shown for curiosity and worth 0 points. A 48-buck GPS study found essentially no effect from phase or position.
